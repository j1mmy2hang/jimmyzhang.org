Going from "world → image" (forward optics) is easy and has one clear answer. But going from "image → world" (**reverse** optics) is a nightmare — it's _mathematically ambiguous_. Many different 3D scenes could produce the exact same pattern of light hitting your eye.

Since the math alone can't give a unique answer, your brain uses built-in **assumptions** (shortcuts) to make a "best guess" at what's really out there.

## Probalistic inferences and hidden assumptions

- overhead light source
- things in a shadow are lighter than they appear
- things move in a straight line
- all points on a moving object move together (rigidity)
- occulusion (pacmac vs square)

Vision isn't a passive recording of the world — it's an active inference process. 
Your brain doesn't "see" reality directly; it reconstructs the most probable version of reality using hardwired assumptions, which is why optical illusions can trick these assumptions and reveal how much guessing is really going on.
