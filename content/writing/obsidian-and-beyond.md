## The Browser for Your Own Mind

My friends know I am quite crazy about Obsidian.

I still remember the exhilarating moment when I first discovered Obsidian. My first thought was, damn, this is like a browser of my own. Only that instead of browsing through webpages, I can browse through and edit my own notes. 

Obsidian isn't that of a revolutionary invention like a web browser for sure. But for someone who only knew to take notes by creating Microsoft Word documents and organizing by creating folders in finder, being able to create, navigate, organize and edit my notes so easily all in one place felt so elegant and almost magical. 

Why didn't this exist earlier? Why is something as basic as writing on a laptop still locked behind a Microsoft subscription?

Later, I learned that Obsidian basically copied from coding IDEs like VS Code. Coders have long had the tool for themselves to quickly manage all their scripts, and Obsidian is essentially an IDE rebuilt for markdown and knowledge work.

 It feels almost magical how such a simple markdown note editor is what had always been missing. And that missing piece became infrastructural-level software for millions. 

---

## More Than a Note Editor — the Entire Knowledge Workflow

But Obsidian is more than a markdown note editor. A vibrant plugin ecosystem has grown around it — developers building and sharing small, specific improvements, each making Obsidian more uniquely their own. 

Beyond plugins, Obsidian continues to expand into web clipper, web browser, CLI, sync, publish, and in the future, multi-player collaboration. 

The vision is almost as if to cover the entire area of knowledge workflow from capturing from the internet, taking notes (making computations), collaborating with teams, and finally sharing the outcome through syncing / publishing. 

Obsidian started at the most fundamental step — note-taking, and is quietly and steadily growing outward from there.

## Personal Private Compute and Digital Sovereignty

I came across [Balaji's X post](https://x.com/balajis/status/2016437837013660071) on "Personal Private Compute" and couldn't stop drawing parallels to Obsidian.

The three cores of personal private compute, as summarized by me, are

1. data ownership
2. personal computation / intelligence
3. secure communication

Currently due to digital feudalism, users send their data to cloud servers of big tech companies, let them make the computation, and only return the result, on a UI designed by them. Communicating with other people relies on sending data to centralized servers as well. 

Today, most of our digital lives run on a kind of feudalism — we send data to Big Tech servers, they run the computation, and hand back results through interfaces they control. Privacy statements are theatre. Truly encrypted P2P communication is still the exception, not the norm.

I truly hope Obsidian can push back against this — by keeping notes as local md files (data ownership), by enabling local AI models (Apple is quietly making this more viable with every chip generation), CLI access, and ultimately manual note-taking to do the "computation", and by enabling secure sharing and communication through sync, publish, and in the future, team, and even private messaging. 

Maybe that's a stretch, but a truly exciting one for me — Obsidian not just as a note editor, but as infrastructure for all knowledge work that empower digital agency. 

Or maybe I'm just crazy about Obsidian.
