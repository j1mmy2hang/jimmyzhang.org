title: Tool is not what matters
---
Tool does matter. 

I love spending time selecting, building, and playing with good tools. Holding a well-designed and aesthetic tool in hand makes me happy by nature, even if doing nothing with it. 

Holding an axe in hand makes one feel powerful. 

But fundamentally, when it comes to any type of work, tool isn't what matters the most. 

Ultimately, when it comes to cutting things, it's more about the person than the axe — how much strength they have, how do they use it, what are they using it for. 

---

People come to me asking whether Obsidian or Notion is better. Whether Claude Code or Cursor is bad. 

I always say: there is no right tool — only the tool that fits how *you* work.

If you don't even know what your work is, there's no point talking about tools.

The easiest trap is copying someone else's tool, thinking it gives you their power. It doesn't. Holding a carpenter's axe doesn't make you a carpenter if you don't know how to use it. 

So you copy the methodology too — how you use the tool. The note-taking system. PARA. Zettelkasten. You watch the YouTube video, download the starter pack. YOu tried for a few months and then gave up; something still doesn't click.

This is because above tools and methodologies sits the one thing that actually matters — **mindset**. A deep understanding of yourself and what you're trying to do. Not "which app", "which tool", or "what system" but: *what do I actually want to achieve, and how does this fit my life?*

A good carpenter doesn't only know how to use the axe; they know what they want to make and how to make it.

The MMT Pyramid answers 90% of the questions newcomers ask about tools. The answer is almost always: get clear on **mindset** first. Only then design your methodology. Only then pick your tool. 

Work from the buttom up, not top down. 

Tools are fliud and swapable, methods are similar. New ones come up every day and die the next. Only mindset is truly yours, and that what truly empowers your work.
