Dear friends, 

This month is a pretty travel-heavy one — I first visited Japan (Kyoto and Tokyo), then went to Wuhan, China for my student visa, and finally to Hong Kong for a short trip. And then five days after I came back, I immediately left for Hoi An, Vietnam, where I contributed as a volunteer for [UWC Short Course Doing Good](https://www.forgood.education/doing-good). The experience was so rich and rewarding that I will need several more days to fully process it. But first, on the past month:

I read a book called [[花小钱 得自由]] (*Spending Small Money to Buy Freedom*) by internet minimalism influencer / blogger Naoto Shibuya. If there's one thing that I take from this book it's a refreshed understanding of the relationship between ourselves and the objects we own. For example, one insight that I gained is that before an object is acquired, its exit should be considered and planned in advance. 

Next, two apps. 

First is a new but familiar old friend — [Delphi](https://delphi-web.netlify.app/), but this time on ios. Download [Delphi for ios](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/delphi-think-clearer/id6786626356) here, which is now currently on App Store (US-region only). It is really just Delphi as it is, moved to a new platform, with a fancier UI. It helps you think deeper and know what you want by asking you questions and prompting your thinking. 

Second is something that I am truly excited about — the long awaited for [Intention for mac](https://intention-for-mac.app). It has been on my roadmap forever, and I am glad that I finally made it. 

Intention for Mac is designed to help you use your mac with intention. It asks you to verbalize your intention every time you open your Mac, to prevent mindless usage. It also sends you nudges to make sure you are on track, and friendly suggests that you take a rest when you no longer need to linger on your mac. 

Feel free to download and try it out. Feedback is greatly appreciated. 

Aside, hope you are doing well, wherever you are in the world. Enjoy the last month of the summer. 

Regards,
Jimmy Zhang
